You get one move. Brake, turn… or do nothing. Which decision protects this cargo? Watch both futures. Same starting point. One difference: your intervention.

Twinforge explores the consequences before replaying the scene. Here, one carrier hits a load, which hits another. Without intervention, the largest impact involving the protected equipment reaches one hundred sixteen and a half joules. With the selected command, no contact reaches that equipment in this experiment.

This result is not a hand-picked animation. The software calculates thirty-seven commands: braking, steering, and doing nothing. It looks for the smallest control effort among those meeting the threshold set for this experiment. You can choose a bad command and inspect what changes.

Now delay the move. At a quarter of a second, there is a solution. At half a second, none of the tested commands protects the cargo under our criterion. The collision has already transferred motion. Twinforge also shows when a decision comes too late. This boundary belongs to the tested time grid.

Where does Meta fit? Muse Spark receives the scene and the allowed commands. It proposes a move and explains why. The simulator calculates what actually happens in the model. Spark then receives those measurements and revises its proposal. It does not see the exhaustive search results.

These are the three calls that actually ran. First proposal: one hundred four joules. Failure. Second: eight point five. Still too high. Third: four point six, below the eight-joule threshold. Both failures stay in the journal. About thirty-eight seconds, for less than three US cents.

The local search does better on this small problem: zero joules on the protected cargo, using less control effort. We show that comparison. Meta’s contribution here is a proposal we can challenge, measure, and revise. A persuasive explanation never replaces checking its consequences.
