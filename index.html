HOW THE TEST WORKS

An observer, not an oracle.

  1. The world sends images.

    A separate renderer generates random motion. The observation code consumes timestamped JPEGs. Its input has no positions, velocities, forces, masses or random seed. This is a software data boundary, not a claim of tamper-proof isolation.

  2. SAM 3.1 finds the objects.

    A real Meta request returns segmentation masks. Local visual tracking follows those appearances between SAM updates. Tap or box prompts select a specific object; Hands and By name use concept prompts. The receipt is checked against the exact submitted image hash. Local frames stay on the device between model calls.

  3. Twinforge predicts a future position.

    A separate motion estimator fits past visual observations and forecasts 0.5 to 1 second ahead. The shaded area is a conditional uncertainty estimate, not a guarantee. SAM supplies segmentation; it does not itself predict motion. Every target is stored before its target time.

  4. You lock it. Then reveal it.

    The synthetic world pauses with the target already recorded. Reveal resumes it. The prediction is compared with later tracked pixels. With a live camera, the world cannot be paused: the outcome is recorded automatically. An uploaded video can be paused while its first mask is confirmed.

Change the motion.

The impulse button applies a fresh random force inside the renderer. The observer is not given the force or new velocity. A prediction made before a surprise can fail; the following frames let the tracker adapt.

What the numbers mean.

Errors are 2D image pixels against visual tracking, not independent physical ground truth. The target-time observation may be interpolated between adjacent frames. Occlusion, mistaken identity and abrupt motion can produce errors or an unscorable result. There is no claim of inferred mass or full 3D reconstruction.
